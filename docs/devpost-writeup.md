## Inspiration

In Pew Research Center's 2018 survey of U.S. teens ages 13–17, **17% said they are often or sometimes unable to complete homework because they don't have reliable access to a computer or internet connection**. 12% said they at least sometimes use public Wi-Fi for schoolwork because they have no home internet. And 35% said they often or sometimes do homework on a cellphone, rising to 45% among teens in households earning under $30,000 a year. ([Pew Research Center, 2018](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2018/10/26/nearly-one-in-five-teens-cant-always-finish-their-homework-because-of-the-digital-divide/))

Those students practise on library computers with time limits, shared family phones and old Androids with little data. Many learning tools quietly assume a personal device, an account, and a fast connection. I'm a graduate student in Information Security Policy & Management at Carnegie Mellon's Heinz College, so I also see a privacy problem: logging a child into an account on a public computer isn't a safe default.

I wanted to see how far I could get with the opposite assumptions: **no account, no personal data, no connection needed, and nothing left behind**, without giving up real learning or accessibility.

## What it does

ShareSafe Practice is a tiny offline-first web app for fractions: equivalent fractions, adding and subtracting with unlike denominators, and comparing.

- **Pre-check → practice → post-check.** A 6-question pre-check shows where you start. In practice, each problem has **three progressive hints**, a **fraction-bar visual model**, and **worked steps** on request. A 6-question post-check then shows **before/after** results by skill.
- **No account, no personal data.** Open it and start.
- **Works offline after one visit.** The whole app is about 15 KB gzipped on first load and is cached by a service worker.
- **Leave no trace (on by default).** Progress stays only in the open tab. **End session** wipes everything the app stored (local and session storage, IndexedDB, and optionally the offline copy) and confirms what it removed.
- **Resume code + QR.** An 18-character code like `59N0W7-000011-00082T` restores your exact progress on any device. Scan the QR with your phone before leaving the library, and you can keep going at home without internet.
- **Accessible.** Full keyboard use, visible focus, screen-reader-friendly fractions ("3 over 4"), text size and high-contrast toggles, a **plain-language** toggle for every instruction and hint, **read-aloud**, and no motion.

**A concrete before and after:** a 7th grader with no home internet has 30 minutes on a library PC.

- **Before:** a typical account-based practice site needs an email sign-up first. It stores her session on a shared machine, and it can't be used at home without internet.
- **After:** she starts immediately with no sign-up and takes the pre-check (2/6). She practises with hints, scans her resume code with her phone, and taps **End session** so the library PC keeps nothing. At home she keeps going offline, takes the post-check (5/6), and sees **"Before 2/6 → After 5/6 (+3)"**.

(The scores are illustrative; this exact flow is covered by an automated end-to-end test.)

## How I built it

- **Stack:** TypeScript and Vite with no UI framework, so the bundle stays small. The only runtime library is `qrcode-generator`.
- **Problem engine:** problems are generated procedurally from a seed. The topics and strategies (find the LCD, convert to equivalent fractions, combine the numerators, simplify) follow OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Chapter 4 (CC BY 4.0). Every problem, hint and step is original and generated by code. Every piece of text has a standard version and a plain-language version.
- **Resume code:** 78 bits of progress (a random 16-bit seed plus scores and counters) and a **CRC-10 checksum**, written as 18 Crockford base32 characters. Because problems are deterministic, the code restores the exact next problem without storing any problem content.
- **Honest about security:** the checksum is guaranteed to catch any single wrong character or any two swapped neighbours. It does **not** stop deliberate forgery, because with no server any "secret key" in the page would be public. The app says this plainly. It's fine for self-practice, and teacher-verifiable codes would need a server-held key.
- **Offline:** a post-build script generates a service worker that precaches every built file, versioned by a content hash.
- **Privacy:** sessionStorage by default, no network requests after load, no analytics, and a strict Content-Security-Policy.
- **AI assistance:** I used an AI assistant (Grok Bot) to draft much of the code, tests and documentation. I set the problem, users, scope and design constraints (privacy, honest crypto, accessibility targets) and reviewed the work. The repo README discloses this, and the assistant's commits are labelled.

## Challenges I ran into

- **Offline reloads failing with `net::ERR_FAILED`.** The static server redirected `/index.html` to `/`, and browsers won't use a redirected cached response for a page navigation. Caching the app shell under `/` fixed it.
- **"Tamper-proof" vs. honest.** An HMAC with a key shipped in client code only *looks* secure. I chose a checksum with provable typo detection and documented exactly what it does and doesn't do.
- **A code short enough to write on paper** that still restores exact progress: 18 characters, thanks to seeded generation.
- **Accessibility bugs automation missed.** Pressed toggle buttons were unreadable in high-contrast mode (axe passed them), and initial focus stole the skip link's first Tab stop. Reviewing screenshots and a keyboard-only test caught them.
- **Layout shift** dropped mobile Lighthouse performance to 93. Reserving layout space fixed CLS.

## Accomplishments that I'm proud of

- **Lighthouse 100** for Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO on both mobile and desktop. The first load transfers 18 KiB, and in a throttled test the app is usable in about 1.2 s on emulated 2G.
- **0 axe-core violations** across 16 scans: 8 views, each in default mode and in high-contrast + extra-large text + plain-language mode.
- **31 automated tests**, all passing:
  - 21 unit tests: a 9,000-problem correctness sweep, and exhaustive checks that every single-character typo and every swap of neighbouring characters in a resume code is detected.
  - 10 end-to-end tests: a full keyboard-only journey, offline mode, the QR deep link, and an "End session" test that confirms storage, caches, service workers and IndexedDB are all empty afterwards.
- A complete learning loop with measurable before/after growth, in about 15 KB gzipped.

## What I learned

- Offline-first is all about edge cases (redirects, cache versions, deep links, lazy chunks), so test with the network actually off.
- Privacy is an access feature: "no account, nothing left behind" removes a real barrier on shared devices, and wiping properly means more than `localStorage.clear()`.
- Security claims have to match the threat model. Precisely describing what a checksum guarantees is better than overclaiming.
- Automated accessibility checks are a baseline, not a verdict. Looking at screenshots caught a contrast failure axe didn't.

## What's next for ShareSafe Practice

- Manual testing with NVDA, VoiceOver and TalkBack, on real low-end Android phones, and in Safari/iOS (a checklist is already written).
- More topics (mixed numbers, multiplying and dividing fractions, decimals) and Spanish translations.
- Optional teacher-verifiable codes using a teacher-held signing key, kept separate from the private self-practice mode.
- Pilots with a public library or after-school program, to learn what students on shared computers actually need.
